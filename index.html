Research Profile & Academic Vision

Building Scalable, Efficient & Decentralized Multimodal Foundation AI Systems

I am a Japanese Government (MEXT) Scholar and Graduate Researcher at Iwate Prefectural University, Japan, advised by Prof. Hirokazu Madokoro and Prof. Stephanie Nix. My research investigates the foundational intersection between Machine Learning Algorithms and Systems for AI (SysML).

While multi-billion-parameter Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and LLMs exhibit unprecedented reasoning capabilities, their enormous compute and memory footprints restrict their deployment to centralized, server-grade GPU clusters. My core research vision is to democratize foundation model intelligence through principled algorithmic modularization, dynamic sparsity, deep quantization, and decentralized system execution—enabling multi-modal foundation models to execute reliably on distributed, heterogeneous, and constraint-bound edge devices.

My work spans empirical system implementations, such as MoTE-VLM, a Mixture-of-Tiny-Experts vision-language model whose entire inference runs on a GPU-free Raspberry Pi cluster (25.4 BLEU-4 / 70.4 CIDEr on COCO), and applied work on vision-language analysis of inspection imagery from nuclear decommissioning (supported by JAEA Grant JPJA23O23813888). This research has been recognized with the Best Presentation Award at ACM ICCAI 2026 and the Outstanding Presentation Award at SICE SI2025.

Core Research Dimensions

1. Efficient Multimodal Foundation Models

Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), dynamic soft/hard routing, TinyExpert parameter partitioning, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT/LoRA) for multimodal architectures.

2. Systems for AI & Distributed Inference

Distributed edge orchestration, socket-based inter-node communication protocols, heterogeneous compute scheduling, and memory-bandwidth bottleneck alleviation.

3. Deep Quantization & Model Compression

Post-training quantization (INT8, INT4, sub-byte formats), BitsAndBytes low-bit kernels, knowledge distillation from large teacher VLMs, and token-level pruning.

4. Embodied AI in Degraded Environments

Vision-language understanding of low-light, blurry, degraded imagery for robotic inspection in hazardous environments, motivated by Fukushima Daiichi decommissioning.

MoTE-VLM: A Mixture of Tiny Experts for Vision-Language Inference on Raspberry Pi Clusters

Raspberry Pi 5 · 3–4 Nodes

Vision-language models normally need GPUs and gigabytes of memory. MoTE-VLM replaces the dense feed-forward layers of a transformer with small top-1-routed experts, so that each expert can live on its own single-board computer. The project developed in two stages:

STAGE 1 · ICCAI 2026 (hybrid)

Qwen2.5-VL-3B runs on a workstation GPU while TinyExpert modules (≈2.1M parameters each) run CPU-only on three Raspberry Pi 5 nodes over Gigabit Ethernet. A load-balancing loss keeps expert use within 15% of uniform, and expert computation (not networking) accounts for 81% of latency.

87.24 → 48.55 ms from 1 to 3 nodes (1.80×)

STAGE 2 · JOURNAL (fully GPU-free)

A self-contained 206M-parameter VLM (frozen MobileNetV3-Small encoder, 12 transformer blocks, hash-routed experts) trained on COCO and instruction-tuned on answers distilled from a Qwen3-VL teacher. The entire forward pass runs on a four-node Pi cluster with no GPU.

Key finding: without a GPU, distribution buys memory, not speed

In Stage 1 the GPU made expert computation the bottleneck, so adding Pi nodes reduced latency. In Stage 2 the Pi's own CPU runs the backbone and becomes the bottleneck, so adding nodes adds network round-trips: 392 ms on one node vs. 630 ms on four. With one expert per node, though, no board holds the whole model (the busiest node needs 2.2× fewer parameters), which is what would let a cluster host models too large for any single board. An iso-compute dense baseline matches the mixture's caption quality, confirming that its value here is partitionability rather than accuracy.

Optimization and Implementation of Qwen-VL on Single Board Cluster Computers

Fukushima Daiichi Case Study

Studied edge deployment of Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct for describing low-light, blurry inspection imagery from the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear decommissioning site. Applied BitsAndBytes 8-bit quantization and designed a three-tier master / MoTE-router / Raspberry Pi expert architecture, reducing model memory allocation from 9,344 MB down to 3,915 MB (58% reduction) while preserving descriptive output consistency.

Prospective Ph.D. Research Directions

Open to Global Fall 2027 Admission

For my Ph.D., I want to keep working where machine learning meets systems: making large multimodal models cheap enough to run where they are needed. These are the open problems I would most like to work on:

DIRECTION A

Decentralized Collaborative Foundation Models

Investigating dynamic, serverless multi-agent Foundation Model inference across heterogeneous edge nodes—eliminating centralized parameter servers via peer-to-peer expert routing and fault-tolerant KV caching.

DIRECTION B

Extreme Sub-Byte Quantization for Multimodality

Developing novel non-linear quantization representations (FP4, NF4, 2-bit ternary) tailored specifically to cross-attention layers and vision tokens in multimodal foundation models without perceptual accuracy degradation.

DIRECTION C

Embodied Multimodal Agents & Closed-Loop Control

Bridging efficient VLMs with real-time robotic action policies—enabling constrained autonomous agents to perform visual reasoning, temporal planning, and safety verification directly on edge compute.
