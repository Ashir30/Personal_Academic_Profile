Research Profile & Direction

Advancing Efficient, Distributed Vision-Language Architectures for Constraint-Bound Systems

I am a Japanese Government (MEXT) Scholar and Graduate Researcher at Iwate Prefectural University, advised by Prof. Hirokazu Madokoro and Prof. Stephanie Nix. My research investigates the foundational intersection of Efficient Machine Learning, Distributed Edge AI Systems, and Vision-Language Foundation Models (VLMs).

Modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and LLMs carry multi-billion parameter footprints that restrict them to server-grade GPUs. My research pioneers architectural modularization—specifically Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), TinyExpert partitioning, and dynamic INT8 quantization—to enable foundation model execution across low-cost, CPU-only Single-Board Computer (SBC) clusters with zero GPU acceleration.

My framework, MoTE-VLM, demonstrated the first self-contained, GPU-free vision-language model deployed across a 4-node Raspberry Pi 5 cluster, achieving 25.4 BLEU-4 and 70.4 CIDEr on COCO benchmarks with 48.55 ms latency and a 1.80× cluster speedup. This investigation earned the Best Presentation Award at ACM ICCAI 2026 and an Outstanding Presentation Award at SICE SI2025.

MoTE-VLM: A Mixture of Tiny Experts for GPU-Free Distributed Inference on Single-Board Clusters

206M Parameters · 4-Node Cluster

Vision-Language Models typically require heavy workstation GPUs, placing them out of reach for power- and cost-constrained edge deployments. MoTE-VLM proposes a 206M-parameter architecture with a frozen MobileNetV3-Small encoder emitting 49 spatial vision tokens into a 12-layer transformer where standard FFNs are replaced by a hash-routed Mixture of Tiny Experts (MoTE). With top-1 routing, each token activates only ∼45% of parameters (93M active), allowing each Pi node to host a single expert slice (38 MB at INT8).

Key Systems Insight: The Reversal in the Role of Distribution

Our empirical evaluation revealed that without GPU acceleration, master CPU compute bounds latency (392 ms on 1 node vs 630 ms on 4 nodes due to serialized socket round-trips). Therefore, on CPU-only edge clusters, the primary contribution of MoE distribution is memory partitioning (enabling models too large for a single 8GB board to execute) rather than latency acceleration.

Optimization & Deployment of Qwen-VL on Single-Board Clusters for Extreme Environments

Fukushima Daiichi Case Study

Investigated the practical edge deployment of Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct for real-time robotic inspection under low-light, blurry conditions from the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Reactor decommissioning site. Implemented BitsAndBytes 8-bit quantization and a 3-tier master-worker architecture, reducing model memory allocation from 9,344 MB down to 3,915 MB (58% reduction) while preserving descriptive output consistency.
