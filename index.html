Research Profile & Academic Vision

Building Scalable, Efficient & Decentralized Multimodal Foundation AI Systems

I am a Japanese Government (MEXT) Scholar and Graduate Researcher at Iwate Prefectural University, Japan, advised by Prof. Hirokazu Madokoro and Prof. Stephanie Nix. My research investigates the foundational intersection between Machine Learning Algorithms and Systems for AI (SysML).

While multi-billion-parameter Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and LLMs exhibit unprecedented reasoning capabilities, their enormous compute and memory footprints restrict their deployment to centralized, server-grade GPU clusters. My core research vision is to democratize foundation model intelligence through principled algorithmic modularization, dynamic sparsity, deep quantization, and decentralized system execution—enabling multi-modal foundation models to execute reliably on distributed, heterogeneous, and constraint-bound edge devices.

My work spans empirical system implementations, such as MoTE-VLM (Mixture-of-Tiny-Experts running across GPU-free Raspberry Pi clusters, achieving 25.4 BLEU-4 and 70.4 CIDEr), and mission-critical applications including real-world robotic visual inspection in nuclear decommissioning environments (funded by the Japan Atomic Energy Agency, Grant JPJA23O23813888). This research has been recognized with the Best Presentation Award at ACM ICCAI 2026 and the Outstanding Presentation Award at SICE SI2025.

Core Research Dimensions

1. Efficient Multimodal Foundation Models

Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), dynamic soft/hard routing, TinyExpert parameter partitioning, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT/LoRA) for multimodal architectures.

2. Systems for AI & Distributed Inference

Distributed edge orchestration, socket-based inter-node communication protocols, heterogeneous compute scheduling, and memory-bandwidth bottleneck alleviation.

3. Deep Quantization & Model Compression

Post-training quantization (INT8, INT4, sub-byte formats), BitsAndBytes low-bit kernels, knowledge distillation from large teacher VLMs, and token-level pruning.

4. Embodied AI & Degraded Robotics Diagnostics

Vision-Language diagnostics for autonomous robotic teleoperation under radiation, low-light, and harsh degraded sensors (e.g., Fukushima Daiichi reactor inspection).

MoTE-VLM: A Mixture of Tiny Experts for GPU-Free Distributed Inference on Single-Board Clusters

206M Parameters · 4-Node Cluster

Vision-Language Models typically require heavy workstation GPUs, placing them out of reach for power- and cost-constrained edge deployments. MoTE-VLM proposes a 206M-parameter architecture with a frozen MobileNetV3-Small encoder emitting 49 spatial vision tokens into a 12-layer transformer where standard FFNs are replaced by a hash-routed Mixture of Tiny Experts (MoTE). With top-1 routing, each token activates only ∼45% of parameters (93M active), allowing each Pi node to host a single expert slice (38 MB at INT8).

Key Systems Insight: The Reversal in the Role of Distribution

Our empirical evaluation revealed that without GPU acceleration, master CPU compute bounds latency (392 ms on 1 node vs 630 ms on 4 nodes due to serialized socket round-trips). Therefore, on CPU-only edge clusters, the primary contribution of MoE distribution is memory partitioning (enabling models too large for a single 8GB board to execute) rather than latency acceleration.

Optimization and Implementation of Qwen-VL on Single Board Cluster Computers

Fukushima Daiichi Case Study

Investigated the practical edge deployment of Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct for real-time robotic inspection under low-light, blurry conditions from the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Reactor decommissioning site. Implemented BitsAndBytes 8-bit quantization and a 3-tier master-worker architecture, reducing model memory allocation from 9,344 MB down to 3,915 MB (58% reduction) while preserving descriptive output consistency.
