Research Interests

I study how to make large multimodal models run where they are needed: on small, distributed, resource-constrained hardware. My work so far combines Mixture-of-Experts design, quantization, and distributed edge inference for vision-language models, measured on real single-board-computer clusters. For my Ph.D. I am interested in:

  • Efficient multimodal foundation models — sparse Mixture-of-Experts, routing, parameter-efficient adaptation, knowledge distillation.
  • Systems for ML — distributed and decentralized inference across heterogeneous edge devices; memory, communication, and scheduling bottlenecks.
  • Model compression — low-bit quantization for vision-language models, especially for cross-attention and vision tokens.
  • Embodied AI — efficient vision-language perception for robots operating in degraded or hazardous environments.

Research Experience

Graduate Researcher — Madokoro & Nix LaboratorySep 2024 – Present
Iwate Prefectural University, Morioka, Japan
MoTE-VLM: a fully GPU-free vision-language model on a Raspberry Pi cluster [J1]
  • Designed and trained a 206M-parameter VLM (frozen MobileNetV3-Small encoder with 49 spatial tokens, 12 transformer blocks, hash-routed Mixture of Tiny Experts) whose entire inference runs CPU-only on a four-node Raspberry Pi 5 cluster, one expert per node.
  • Two-stage training: caption alignment on COCO, then instruction tuning on answers distilled from a Qwen3-VL teacher. Reached 25.4 BLEU-4 / 70.4 CIDEr on held-out COCO val2017 with no GPU at inference.
  • Ran controlled studies: a parameter-free hash router outperformed a learned router (70.4 vs. 45.7 CIDEr; the learned router collapsed onto one or two experts), and an iso-compute dense baseline matched the mixture's quality (paired bootstrap), showing that the mixture's value is memory partitioning rather than accuracy.
  • Characterized deployment trade-offs: INT8 post-training quantization made the model 2.7× smaller (309 MB) but only 1.1× faster on the Pi CPU (vs. 4.0× on x86); without a GPU, latency rose from 392 ms (1 node) to 630 ms (4 nodes), so distribution buys memory, not speed.
Distributed tiny experts for Qwen2.5-VL-3B on single-board computers [C2]
  • Built a hybrid edge pipeline: Qwen2.5-VL-3B on a workstation GPU, with ~2.1M-parameter TinyExpert modules executing CPU-only on three Raspberry Pi 5 nodes over TCP/Gigabit Ethernet with top-1 softmax routing and a load-balancing loss.
  • Reduced expert-stage latency from 87.24 ms (1 node) to 48.55 ms (3 nodes), a 1.80× speedup; expert computation accounted for 81% of latency and expert load stayed within 15% of uniform.
Quantized VLM for nuclear decommissioning imagery [C1]
  • Applied BitsAndBytes 8-bit quantization to Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, reducing memory allocation from 9,344 MB to 3,915 MB, and evaluated descriptions of low-light, blurry inspection images from the Fukushima Daiichi decommissioning site. Supported by JAEA Grant JPJA23O23813888.

Across all projects I carried out the literature review, system design, implementation, experiments, and LaTeX manuscript preparation; the move to Mixture-of-Experts came from my finding that dense deployment was infeasible on Pi hardware.
